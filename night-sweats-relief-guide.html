5 Practical Ways to Help Stop Night Sweats From Ruining Your Sleep

You wake up at 3 a.m., soaked through your sheets, heart racing, wide awake — again. Night sweats are one of the most disruptive parts of menopause, and they don't just affect sleep. They affect your mood, your energy, and your ability to function the next day. Here's what may actually help.

1. Layer Your Bedding, Not Your Body
Moisture-wicking sheets and a lightweight, breathable blanket let you regulate temperature quickly without fully waking up to strip off layers.
2. Keep a Cooling Tool Within Reach
A bedside fan, cooling pillow, or gel mat can bring your body temperature down fast the moment a hot flash starts.
3. Watch Your Evening Triggers
Alcohol, caffeine, and spicy food in the evening are commonly linked to more frequent or intense night sweats for many women.
4. Support Hormonal Balance With Nutrition
Some women find that magnesium, black cohosh, or phytoestrogen-rich foods may help ease the frequency of hot flashes over time.
5. Build a Wind-Down Routine
Stress and poor sleep hygiene can intensify night sweats. A consistent, calming bedtime routine may help regulate your body's overnight response.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are night sweats worse than daytime hot flashes?
Body temperature naturally shifts during sleep cycles, which can make hormonal fluctuations feel more intense at night and disrupt deeper stages of sleep.
Do cooling products actually work?
Many women report real comfort improvements from moisture-wicking fabrics and bedside cooling tools, though individual results vary.
When should I talk to a doctor about night sweats?
If night sweats are frequent, severe, or affecting your quality of life, a healthcare provider can help explore options including lifestyle changes or medical treatment.
